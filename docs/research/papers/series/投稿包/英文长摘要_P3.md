## Extended Abstract

The classification of English motion verbs has long oscillated between Levin's alternation-based, lemma-level verb classes and the frame-level or topic-domain partitions offered by FrameNet and WordNet Domains. All three resources are indispensable, yet none models how individual senses of a highly polysemous verb (run has 39 senses in OALD10, go 60, fly 35) distribute across motion-event types. This paper reports the third study of a series on the schema-based categorization of motion verbs and constructs a two-level, sense-based classification — domains and families — that is schema-driven, empirically generated and fully traceable.

The empirical basis is 171 lemmas retained from Levin's (1993) Chapter 51 after dual verification (136 manner-focusing, 22 path-focusing, 13 interaction-focusing) and their 996 OALD10 senses. Three macro-categories and twelve subcategories are established at the lemma level, the interaction-focusing category being an empirical contribution of the series: chase-type and accompany-type verbs profile neither manner nor path in the verb root but a spatial relation to another participant. Cause is kept strictly at the event-structure level, and causativity is handled at the sense level, so that category labels and feature columns cannot contaminate each other. The domain–family system is imposed only on Y-senses (core physical-displacement senses); metaphorical extensions are archived in radial networks attached to their source senses.

The system is generated, not presupposed. Four rules govern generation: the label inventory is fixed in advance as a theoretical commitment, but membership is a data decision; aggregation precedes naming; every family name must be supported by a feature profile and representative examples; and the merging of goal-arrival and source-detachment into the path domain remains a testable hypothesis rather than a verdict. A mechanical candidate layer (992 → 358 senses through ten positive and seventeen negative regular-expression signal classes) and an explicit screening rule set (six abstract senses removed, five metaphorical-but-viable senses explicitly retained, three boundary cases queued for review) yield the v0.2 pre-mapping of 352 senses across three domains and twelve families: manner-displacement 264, path-displacement 62, interactional-displacement 26.

A vertical case study of the RUN category supplies the full sense-level empirical chain. After three-state discrimination (23 English lemmas and 32 target senses; 24 Chinese entries and 25 sense items, retained 14 / peripheral 5 / excluded 6), target senses were coded on fifteen P/M/C parameters (path, manner, cause; three verification-only columns excluded from the matrix), clustered separately for each language by Gower distance with average linkage at an exploratory k = 5 (cophenetic correlation: English 0.825, Chinese 0.795), and aligned across languages by cluster-center distance with a four-way verdict: one fully aligned pair, three partially aligned pairs, one zero-aligned pair. The manner-of-running category aligns fully across languages (distance 0.045), while the fleeing schema splits in Chinese into a directed and a scattering escape family — a morphological splitting of the "tao-X" word family that English, relying on monomorphemic flee/escape/bolt, does not show. Four contrast cases against Levin's Chapter 51 (sense-level exclusion of race, gold-standard gaps such as sprint and stampede, cluster reassignment of bound, and the word-formation contrast) demonstrate that lemma-level classes systematically misrepresent polysemous lemmas.

The domain–family system is complementary to Levin classes, FrameNet frames and WordNet Domains — construction tests guard lemma admission, frames supply scene evidence, topic domains serve retrieval, and domains-and-families organize senses by image schema — and directly supports the macrostructure of bilingual learners' dictionaries: domains as part divisions, families as navigation units, senses as the kernel of entries, with cross-language alignment verdicts marking where translation equivalence must be explicitly taught rather than assumed.

**Keywords**: motion verbs; domain-family system; schema-based categorization; sense unit; hierarchical clustering; English-Chinese alignment; learners' dictionaries
