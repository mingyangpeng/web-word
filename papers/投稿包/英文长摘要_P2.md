## Extended Abstract

Image schemas are the central analytical unit of motion-event semantics in cognitive linguistics, yet their attribution has long rested on the researcher's introspection: to say that run profiles a manner-accompaniment schema or that depart profiles a source-detachment schema is to assert, not to show. This paper reports the second study of a series on the schema-based categorization of English motion verbs and describes a procedure that makes schema attribution corpus-verifiable and item-by-item checkable.

The evidence base is Sketch Engine word-sketch data from enTenTen21 (approx. 52 billion words, 61.6 billion tokens), retrieved on 30 September 2026 with verb part-of-speech restriction and fifty collocates per grammatical slot: 50,751 rows of collocation detail for the 136 manner-focusing motion verbs (69,943 rows after the lemma list was extended to 171). A five-step procedure links raw collocation to schema labels: (i) a schema-by-slot evidence mapping assigns each of four schemas — manner-accompaniment, source-detachment, goal-arrival, interactional-motion — the grammatical slots in which its defining element can surface; (ii) a schema prominence index S = Mean_logDice × log2(N+1) ranks candidate schemas per lemma; (iii) lemma-level candidates are screened against the coding manual's initial judgments; (iv) human noise remediation proceeds in three layers (homograph blending, semantic classification of collocates, and definition cross-checking against OALD10); and (v) the final forced-choice schema label is assigned at the sense level, with secondary schemas recorded for radial-network filing.

The lemma-level screening distribution is extremely skewed — 118 lemmas as manner-accompaniment, 10 as goal-arrival, 7 as interactional-motion, 1 as source-detachment — and this skew is itself a methodological finding. Layered evidence-chain analyses of three noise cases show why statistical clusters are not linguistic sense classes: tear's goal-arrival candidacy arises from homograph blending with the tearing verb (veil, hair, wall in the objects-of-away/off/down slots encode separation and demolition, not motion); sweep's source-detachment candidacy arises from metaphorical sweeping uses (foot in sweep someone off their feet, mop and vacuum in the coordinated-verb slot expose the housework semantic field); and ferry's interactional candidacy arises from pseudo-interaction object slots (passengers and cargo are transported, not co-moving interactors). From these cases the paper distills a three-signal testability criterion — slot coverage, semantic homogeneity of classified collocates, and dictionary-definition cross-checking — no single signal being sufficient for a schema label.

The restoration of 35 path- and interaction-focusing lemmas to the lemma list supplies the genuine members without which the four-schema system cannot stand: eight source-class lemmas for source-detachment, fourteen goal-class lemmas for goal-arrival, and seven purpose-class plus six co-participant lemmas for interactional motion. Shared schemas are then induced across verbs: lemmas sharing a schema may have structurally different evidence profiles (walk relies on subjects and modifiers, limp on the definition, run on the prominence index and coordinated-verb slots; arrive anchors GOAL in prepositional slots while reach anchors it in object slots), but after semantic classification their element orientation must be isomorphic and the definition check must agree.

Shared schemas thus form an intermediate representational layer between sense-admission adjudication upstream and the domain–family classification downstream, directly supporting definition generalization, discrimination and evidence-ranked example selection in learners' dictionaries.

**Keywords**: image schema; motion verbs; word sketch; collocational evidence; shared schema; sense-level annotation; learners' dictionary definitions
